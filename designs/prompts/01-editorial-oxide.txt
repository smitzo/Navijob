Use case: ui-mockup.
Create one high-fidelity desktop website UI prototype for NaviJob, a serious Indian recruitment and jobs application platform. This is a flat, front-facing screenshot of the actual job discovery page, not a device mockup or a marketing poster. Landscape canvas around 1536 by 1024. Make small text sharp and readable with believable information hierarchy and carefully aligned grids.
Design direction: an established careers publication with editorial authority, drawing on the craft of Welcome to the Jungle and The Dots without copying their branding. White and warm paper surfaces, nearly black typography, a single restrained burnt-oxide accent #A84932. Elegant compact editorial serif for the headline, a strong humanist sans for every functional label. Type is the identity. Restrained square-cornered controls, very subtle dividers, generous margins and precise spacing.
Content: top wordmark "navijob" and navigation "Find jobs", "Companies", "My profile", "Applications", with a quiet "For employers" link. A modest headline "Find work worth doing." A search row for "Role or keyword" and "Location". Below it a functional recruitment page with a left filter column headed "Filter jobs" with Role, Location, Work mode, Experience, Salary; a wide central list of four job rows; and a narrow editorial company spotlight with an authentic-looking small office photograph, caption and company details. No giant hero taking over the useful page.
Use fictional sample companies and realistic rows: "Backend Engineer" at "Morrow Labs", "Bengaluru · Hybrid", "₹18–28 LPA", "2–4 years"; "Full-stack Engineer" at "Parcel Works", "Remote · India", "₹15–24 LPA"; "Software Engineer" at "Ledger House", "Pune · On-site", "₹12–20 LPA"; "Platform Engineer" at "Northline", "Remote · India", "₹22–35 LPA". Each row has a small monogram company mark, clear hierarchy, a save icon and restrained metadata, separated by fine rules. One row can be selected with a tiny oxide indicator. Footer note "Design concept · Sample roles".
Everything should feel deliberate, mature, inviting and highly usable. Do not show AI agents, chatbots, match percentages, fake user counts, fake employer endorsements, enormous rounded cards, rainbow gradients, purple glows, glassmorphism, floating illustrations, bento tiles, emoji icons, dashboard analytics or futuristic effects. This must look like a real recruitment product designed by an experienced editorial product designer.
